# Trend

How something moved and what moved it: the series itself, from its source, with the events
that explain its turns marked on it.

## Collect

- The series from the publisher (`fetch.mjs`, or a table on its site), over a window at least
  three times as long as the period asked about, so "recently" can be seen against normal.
- A comparison series when it tells the story: the same measure elsewhere, the thing it is
  usually compared with (rent against pay, a stock against its index). Different units:
  `--index`.
- For each turn the text explains, a dated event from a source: a rate decision, a policy, a
  launch, a shock. A turn with no sourced cause is said to be unexplained.

## Final text

The level now and its date; the change over the period asked about, as a number and a percent;
the one or two things that moved it. Missing or revised points are said.

## Page

1. Title: the movement as a finding. Lede: level now, change, main cause.
2. The line chart, with a `--mark` for each event the text names.
3. A small table: now, one year ago, three or five years ago, the high and low with dates.
4. What moved it, one short paragraph per mark, each with its source.
5. What to watch: the next release date, and the level that would change the story.

## Example (illustrative numbers)

> **Mortgage rates fell a full point in a year, following the 10-year bond**
>
> The 30-year rate is 6.1% (2026-09-17), down from 7.1% a year earlier; the 10-year Treasury
> fell from 4.6% to 3.9% over the same weeks.
>
> [chart: both lines since 2020, marks at the first rate cut and the jobs report that followed]
>
> Watch: the next inflation release on 10-14; a monthly core reading above 0.3% has pushed the
> 10-year up each time this year.
