# Comparison with a pick

Options side by side — products, plans, cities, schools, companies — ending in one pick for what
the user said matters.

## Collect

- The options: the ones the user named, exactly those. Asked to choose them, three to five that a
  person shopping today would really consider, and say why those.
- The criteria: what the user said matters first, then what decides this kind of choice (price
  over the time it is owned, not only the sticker; the spec people regret skipping).
- Every cell from a page you opened, all on the same basis: the same period, the same definition,
  one currency (say the exchange rate and its date). A cell you could not fill is "—" with the
  reason in a note, never blank and never a guess.

## Final text

The pick and the one number that decides it, then the runner-up and when it wins instead
("Take B; if you fly more than twice a year, A pays for itself"). The page's path.

## Page

1. Title: the pick as a finding. Lede: the pick, its deciding number, and who should choose
   otherwise.
2. The matrix: options as columns (or rows, when there are more than four), criteria as rows,
   units in the row label, the best cell in each row marked, the pick's column marked. A photo
   of each option when people choose it partly by how it looks.
3. One bar chart for the criterion that decides it (`--highlight` the pick).
4. Per option, two lines: what it is best at, what it gives up.
5. Sources and the date checked.

## Example (illustrative numbers)

> **Plan B costs the least for a family of four** — ₩118,000 a month against ₩131,000 and ₩146,000.
>
> | | Plan A | **Plan B** | Plan C |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Monthly, 4 lines (₩) | 131,000 | **118,000** | 146,000 |
> | Data per line (GB) | 30 | 25 | **unlimited** |
> | Contract (months) | 24 | **none** | 24 |
>
> Plan C wins only if two or more of you use more than 25 GB most months.
